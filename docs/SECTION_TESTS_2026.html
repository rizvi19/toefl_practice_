Format, sources & practice limits

This is independent practice for the regular TOEFL iBT format introduced on 21 January 2026. It is not an ETS product, an exact replica of its interface, or a calibrated exam.

Reading and Listening are two-stage adaptive sections. The second module depends on performance in the first. Actual times and item counts may vary. The published blueprint includes unscored items and notes that its pre-launch specifications could undergo minor revisions. Its base figures agree with the current ETS content page.

Reading uses Complete the Words, Read in Daily Life and Read an Academic Passage. Listening uses Listen and Choose a Response, Listen to a Conversation, Listen to an Announcement and Listen to an Academic Talk. The current Listening page contains an introductory count typo but lists all four tasks; the content table and specification document agree on four.

Reading permits Back, Next and Review within a module, but you cannot return to Module 1 after starting Module 2. Listening questions must be answered in order; you cannot return or replay audio. Help does not stop the clock. These navigation rules are confirmed by the user-supplied ETS Pocket Edition guide, toolbar guidance on page 15, Reading timing on page 84 and Listening directions on page 260. The supplied screenshots and video transcripts were also inspected; their variable question totals are not used as fixed official counts.

How this site simulates the test

  • Reading: 35 items in Module 1, then 15 in Module 2. Module 1 has 20 word gaps, 10 everyday questions and 5 academic questions. Upper Module 2 has 10 gaps and 5 academic questions; lower has 10 gaps and 5 everyday questions.
  • Listening: 32 items in Module 1, then 15 in Module 2. Upper Module 2 includes an academic talk. Lower Module 2 uses everyday listening tasks. These are training forms, not a guarantee about every ETS form.
  • Module 1 accuracy of at least 70% selects the upper route; otherwise the lower route. This threshold is our transparent training choice, not an ETS threshold. Reading also offers fixed upper/lower routes and five layouts from the supplied screenshot. Their totals vary. Difficulty tags use a relative editorial complexity rubric and have not been psychometrically calibrated.
  • Reading defaults to 30 minutes, split 21:00 / 9:00. Module 1 time and task counts can be configured independently. Listening defaults to 29 minutes, split 18:00 / 11:00 on the upper route or 18:00 / 7:00 on the lower route. Custom settings scale these budgets. Unused time does not carry over.
  • Directions are untimed. Once started, the practice clock runs continuously, including narration, module-transition screens, Help, confirmations and time away from the page. This is an elapsed-time practice clock; it does not claim to reproduce every ETS clock behavior.
  • Listening uses a Play button for browser compatibility. Choices appear after audio ends. Each stimulus is heard once, its questions appear one at a time, and responses lock with Next. Reloading during playback permits an interrupted-audio recovery and marks the report as nonstandard practice.
  • Available bundled recordings play as real audio. Other items use synthetic browser English voices; conversations select two distinct voices when available. Voice quality and accents depend on the device. Browser voice service may require internet. The legacy library retains its optional Kokoro controls.
  • Reading word gaps have fixed slots with keyboard and paste support. Everyday question groups stay together; academic questions appear one at a time alongside the full passage.

Local estimated 1–6 practice scores

ETS reports official section scores on a 1–6 scale in half-point increments from January 2026. This local model matches only that reporting scale. The published legacy-score comparison table is not a conversion from raw accuracy on this bank.

Every question and word gap receives one practice point. Unanswered items count as incorrect. The estimate is 1 + 5 × (correct items / total items), rounded to the nearest 0.5 and restricted to 1–6. For example, 40/50 yields 5.0 with this practice formula. Ended-early attempts and new Reading attempts that have not reached Module 2 have answer review but no estimated score. Task and skill feedback reports use the actual configured item counts.

This is not an official ETS conversion, a validated proficiency measurement, a CEFR result, or a prediction of your TOEFL score. ETS uses calibrated adaptive scoring, including item characteristics and unscored content; accuracy alone cannot reproduce it. This site shows a separate section estimate and does not invent an overall score from two sections.

Question bank and copyright

Reading uses 222 distinct activities: 100 Complete the Words, 30 Daily Life Short, 30 Daily Life Long, 30 Text Chain and 32 Academic Passage activities, with 1,400 scored items. These daily-life variants belong to one official task type. Exact and near-duplicate passages were removed under documented audit criteria. Listening retains its original 800 activities. Reading forms avoid repeats within a form and prefer unseen material in the selected pool across attempts; once a pool is exhausted, least-used, oldest items return. Only displayed activities enter the shared section/library history. This browser-local history is not synchronized across devices. See the Reading release guide and audit.

The uploaded Complete the Words worksheets, Essential Exercise TOEFL 2026, TOEFL Preparation Book 2026–2027 and ETS Pocket Guide were read as format references. None of their protected passages, questions, transcripts, answer keys, illustrations or recordings were added to the published site. The uploaded PDFs, screenshots and transcripts remain private reference material and unchanged. Existing publisher references do not imply endorsement.
